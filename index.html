Eleven thousand meters of ocean, from sunlight to permanent dark. Scroll down — the deeper you go, the less light comes with you.

Scroll to dive
0–200m · Sunlit zone

Where the light still reaches.

Ninety percent of ocean life lives here, in the epipelagic zone, where sunlight still drives photosynthesis and reef fish move in loose, glittering schools.

200–1,000m · Twilight zone

Blue fades to nothing.

The mesopelagic zone gets just enough light to see by, not enough to grow by. Jellyfish drift here in slow, deliberate pulses, built for a world with nowhere to hide.

1,000–4,000m · Midnight zone

Total dark. Every light here is alive.

No sunlight reaches the bathypelagic zone at all. Everything that glows down here made its own light — a lure, a warning, or a question no one answers twice.

4,000–6,000m · The abyss

Pressure enough to fold steel.

At the abyssopelagic zone, water pressure exceeds 400 atmospheres. Almost nothing lives here — almost. What does has no eyes left to lose.

6,000–11,000m · The trench

The floor of the world.

This is the Mariana Trench's Challenger Deep — the deepest point on Earth. Fewer people have stood here than have walked on the Moon.

You've gone further than sunlight ever has.

Eleven thousand meters down, and the ocean still isn't finished. Head back up whenever you're ready.
